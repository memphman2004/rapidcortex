# Using the Maplewood County demo

This is for sales, marketing, and anyone presenting NexCort iQ. The demo is a fictional communications center. It is not connected to a live 911 center, and it does not contain real callers, real dispatchers, or real incidents.

The amber bar at the top of the screen always reads:

**DEMO MODE — Fictional Data Only — Not Connected to Live 911 Operations**

Leave that bar in every recording and every live presentation. It cannot be closed.

## Which scenario to use

| If you are talking with… | Open this scenario | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A general audience, a first meeting, or a website video | Standard Operations | A normal morning. Shows calls, Call Assist, transcription, Spanish translation, the incident story, the supervisor view, and units moving through the call. |
| A center that is worried about Friday nights or call volume | High-Volume Night | Four dispatchers, a growing queue, a priority call that jumps the line, and a repeat location. This is the Call Assist story under pressure. |
| A supervisor, a commander, or a director | Mass Casualty Response | An interstate crash and a building fire at the same time. Shows the major-incident flag, coverage gaps, air medical, and command notes. |
| A small center with two or three people on overnight | Overnight Minimum Staffing | 2:00 a.m., two dispatchers, a supervisor watching from home. Call Assist handles the non-emergency calls so the medical call is not interrupted. |
| An investor pitch, a screen recording, or a non-technical audience | Guided Product Tour | Nothing moves until you click Next. You control the pace. |

## What each scenario is meant to show

- **Standard Operations** — the product on an ordinary shift.
- **High-Volume Night** — why Call Assist and the supervisor board matter when the queue gets deep.
- **Mass Casualty Response** — command visibility when every unit is committed.
- **Overnight Minimum Staffing** — the staffing and overtime story for a small center.
- **Guided Product Tour** — one feature at a time, in your own words.

## During a live demo

1. Open the demo. You will see five cards. Pick one and click **Load Scenario**.
2. The board fills in as Maplewood County. Press **Play** if it does not start on its own.
3. The control tab is a thin **Demo** label on the right edge. Click it, or press **Ctrl+Shift+D** (on a Mac, **Command+Shift+D**).
4. From the panel you can pause, play, reset, or run at 1×, 2×, or 4×.
5. Hide the panel again before you record. The amber bar stays. The controls should not be in the shot.
6. **Minimal** in the panel leaves only play, pause, and speed if you need them while you talk.

The event list inside the panel is for you. It is not part of the product screen. Use it to know what is about to happen so you can talk over it.

## Suggested things to say

**Standard Operations.** “This is a normal morning. The chest-pain call brings up guidance before the dispatcher has to go hunting for it. A Spanish-speaking caller stays on the line with the dispatcher. The incident writes its own timeline.”

**High-Volume Night.** “It is Friday. The queue is already holding. A priority medical call does not wait behind a dog complaint. Call Assist takes the non-emergency call. The supervisor can see who is overloaded.”

**Mass Casualty Response.** “Two major incidents at once. The supervisor can see that every ambulance is committed, ask for the helicopter, and write a command note without leaving the screen.”

**Overnight Minimum Staffing.** “Two people are in the room. The road-condition call never reaches them. The breathing call still gets the right guidance and an ambulance. A supervisor at home can look in.”

**Guided Product Tour.** Read the sentence on the callout, then go to the next stop. Do not rush. The audience only needs one idea at a time.

## Guided tour

1. Load **Guided Product Tour**. It does not play by itself.
2. Open the controls and click **Next highlight**, or press the **right arrow** key when you are not typing in a box.
3. You can also jump straight to a stop by name.
4. Each stop dims the rest of the screen and points at one area. Click **Next** on the callout to move on.
5. Moving through the tour does not fast-forward a scenario. They are separate.

An administrator can change the sentence on a stop from the Admin section of the control panel. That change is for the current demo session. It does not require a software release.

## Sending a link to a prospect

A prospect does not need an account.

1. Open the demo controls and the Admin section.
2. Choose the scenario.
3. Put your name in the issuer box so the team can tell who sent the link.
4. Click **Create 7-day share link**.
5. Copy the link and text or email it.

The link opens that scenario with the amber bar showing. It stops working after seven days. An administrator can turn a single link off, or turn off every link from one sender.

If the link says it is expired, revoked, or not valid, the prospect sees only that message. They do not see the product, and they do not see a live center.

Creating links requires the signing secret to be set up by engineering. If the button says the secret is not configured, ask engineering. Do not invent a password or paste a key into a slide.

## If the demo stalls or the browser is slow

- Click **Pause**, then **Play**.
- If the board looks wrong, click **Reset**. The shift returns to the first moment of the scenario.
- If the whole page is stuck, refresh the browser and load the scenario again.
- Use 1× for a recording. Use 2× only when you are walking someone through it live and you need to reach the next moment.
- Close extra browser tabs. The demo runs in the browser. It does not call a live center, so a slow page is a computer problem, not a 911 problem.

## Starting over in the middle of a meeting

Click **Reset**. Say, “Let me take that back to the start of the shift.” Then press **Play**.

To leave the demo, use **End this demo session** in the Admin section. The fictional board is cleared from that browser tab.
