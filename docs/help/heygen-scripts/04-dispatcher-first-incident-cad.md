## Narration (HeyGen)

This video walks one incident from intake toward CAD — the way NexCort i-Q actually works in production agencies.

Select the call in your queue, or create the incident per your agency workflow. Confirm the transcript is flowing. Review the triage snapshot and confidence. Re-run analysis if the story changes.

Open SOP Protocol coaching when it appears. Follow, skip, or override. Agency SOP always wins. Log material deviations in incident notes when policy requires.

Add clear, factual updates: what you verified, actions you took, changes since the last note. Do not speculate.

For CAD: use the CAD entry tools your agency enabled. Many deployments start with read or copy-assist — you still enter or submit into CAD under your agency’s rules. Incident create and update write-back stay fail-closed until your agency completes U-A-T and written authorization. If write-back is off, that is by design — not a broken button.

If your agency requires supervisor approval before write-back, expect an approval queue on the supervisor side. Do not invent an approval to force write-back.

Location, nature, and comments must match the call. Verify Pinpoint or A-L-I against what the caller says. When the incident is ready under SOP, complete your CAD path and stay with the caller until transfer or close rules say otherwise.

NexCort i-Q assists. You remain the authority. CAD and radio remain systems of record.
